Economy
Australian inflation jumps to 4.9%, led by fuel and electricity
The ABS says consumer prices rose 4.9% in the year to October, up from 4.3% and well above the RBA's 2-3% target. Fuel was up about 33% over the year and electricity 25% as government rebates ended. The RBA's preferred trimmed mean measure rose to 3.8% from 3.6%, and building costs rose 5.1%. Treasurer Jim Chalmers called it "a very serious moment". Economists at KPMG and NAB expect the RBA to hold at its 7-8 December meeting, with NAB still expecting cuts from August; the IMF urged the RBA to stay ready to tighten if prices worsen. The IMF also expects growth to slow to 1.9% in 2026 and 1.6% in 2027.
Why it matters to you: Rent, mortgage and petrol pressure is not easing yet, but most economists see this as a peak rather than the start of another rate hike.
Sources: The Guardian · WSJ · Bloomberg
Economy
Unemployment at 4.9%, the highest since 2021
Australia's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate rose to 4.9% in October, the highest since October 2021, ABS data show. Employment fell by 44,000 and unemployment rose by 18,000, while hours worked fell 0.2%. Chalmers has warned unemployment will pass 5% and is due to deliver the mid-year budget update in about three weeks, promising substantial savings. Analysts say the Treasurer must trim spending without tipping a weakening economy into recession.
Why it matters to you: Falling inflation relief plus a softer jobs market makes entry-level and graduate hiring harder, and raises the odds of rate cuts next year.
Sources: Fibre2Fashion (ABS data) · The Conversation

Wall Street rebounds after an AI-led sell-off
The S&P 500 fell 8.6% in the week to 20 November, led by AI stocks, then gained 3.9% through Wednesday, on track for its best Thanksgiving week since 2008 if Friday's half-day session holds. US markets were shut Thursday and US futures point sharply higher. The VIX fear gauge peaked near 36 on 20 November and was still about 26 on Tuesday. Nvidia has reportedly halted chip shipments to south-east Asia, and the Bank of England warned of growing financial risks from opaque AI borrowing.
Why it matters to you: Index funds and super funds hold heavy AI exposure, so this volatility reaches ordinary portfolios.

Alzheimer's drug diranersen slows decline 38% in phase 2 extension; FDA clears Neuralink pivotal trial
In the long-term extension of the phase 2 CELIA trial, Biogen's diranersen (BIIB080), a twice-yearly injection, slowed clinical decline by 38% on the CDR-SB scale at 96 weeks at the 60 mg dose, up from 26% at 76 weeks. Patients still declined, only more slowly, and larger trials are needed. Separately, the FDA approved Neuralink's pivotal study, the first US pivotal trial of an implantable brain-computer interface.
Why it matters to you: Early-stage results, but a sign that Alzheimer's treatment is broadening beyond current drugs, and brain implants are moving toward real trials.
